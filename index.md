**What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

So far the thing I've actually sat down and started conceptualizing deeply is the difference between harmonic sound and noise. Harmonic sounds (like vowels) 
have repeating patterns, with harmonics and amplitude that make them identifiable as a pitch. Noise, like fricatives ("s", "f", "sh"), doesn't 
have that repeating structure, which is exactly what makes it so difficult to artificially mimic.

**Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

I'd like to look into how well our model handles noisy sounds like fricatives compared to harmonic ones, and whether there's a better way to generate the noise 
part so it sounds more natural. A few backup directions if that doesn't pan out: comparing how different voices or instruments break down into harmonic vs. 
noise parts, or seeing how much the generated audio quality drops when the training data is limited or recorded in noisier environments.

**What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

I would like to form a deeper understanding of what sound is as a whole before jumping into the modeling. I'm still having a bit of a hard time grasping the concept, 
and I think it's because we've only scratched the surface. I'd spend more time early on with sound frequencies and the math behind them, so the modeling choices 
make more sense to me instead of just following the steps.

**What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I'd be interested in using Fourier transforms and spectrograms to actually visualize the harmonic vs. noise parts of a sound, since that would help me see what 
I'm still struggling to picture. I'd also like to try filtered noise generation to better mimic fricatives.
